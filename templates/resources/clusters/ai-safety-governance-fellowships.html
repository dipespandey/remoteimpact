    Programme details checked 28 October 2026. This is a selection of documented routes, not a ranking or a promise of admission. Application windows and conditions change; use each official source before applying. For the wider field, see our AI safety and governance career guide.

    GovAI: UK seasonal fellowships

    GovAI offers research and applied tracks. Its official opportunities page lists the UK Summer Fellowship 2027 in London, from 7 June to 27 August, with a 22 November 2026 application deadline. Check that page for the deadline's timezone and any changes.

    The Applied Track notice describes a full-time, in-person programme with £14,000 pay for the three-month period, travel support and temporary work-visa sponsorship. That amount is programme pay, not an annual salary. Limited remote days for caring responsibilities do not make it a work-from-anywhere placement. The research track focuses on research; applied projects can suit experience in operations, communications and programme management.

    IAPS: a remote option with required travel

    The Institute for AI Policy and Strategy's Spring 2027 fellowship runs from 22 February to 14 May. Participants can choose Washington, DC, London or remote work, but everyone must attend the opening event in DC from 22 February to 5 March. The published full-time stipends are $18,000 for Fellows and $24,000 for Senior Fellows for the three-month programme; the DC and London tracks receive an additional $3,000.

    On this guide's check date, IAPS says Spring 2027 applications are closed. Check its official page for future rounds. IAPS is a different organisation from the AI Policy Institute.

    Horizon: paid placements requiring Washington, DC residence

    The Horizon Fellowship supports placements across government, Congress and think tanks. Its junior think-tank track can suit recent graduates. Initial placements last six months for junior fellows and one year for other fellows, with possible renewal.

    The official eligibility FAQ says placements cannot be done remotely: fellows must live in the Washington, DC area. Existing US work authorisation is required; Horizon does not sponsor employment visas. Remote training or matching does not remove the residence requirement. The 2027 cohort's published 30 August 2026 deadline has passed. Use the official programme page for future application rounds and funding terms.

    Mila: a documented policy-research route

    Mila's 2025–2026 cohort announcement describes six-month policy projects with research advisers. This is evidence of a past programme, not a current application notice or a guarantee that the same format will recur. Its policy conference page also documents an in-person fellowship component. Verify the next official call's experience, funding, travel and attendance requirements before treating it as an accessible remote option.

    How to choose a route

    • Check access first. Read residence, travel, visa and work-authorisation conditions before preparing a long application.
    • Compare funding on the same basis. Separate total programme pay from annual salary, benefits and travel support. Ask about payment timing and upfront costs if they would affect your ability to participate.
    • Match the work to your experience. A research proposal may suit a research track; operations or communications work can be more relevant to an applied track. Follow the actual selection criteria.
    • Keep a small work sample ready. A clear policy memo or analysis can demonstrate your reasoning. Do not assume every programme needs the same proposal or qualification.

    Fellowships are one route, not a prerequisite for meaningful work. Compare early-career opportunities and current AI safety listings. For employer research roles rather than a fixed-duration fellowship, use CSET's official careers page and check the individual role's terms.
