    What are climate and environment jobs?

    Climate and environment roles are positions whose main purpose is to reduce greenhouse-gas emissions, protect ecosystems, or help communities adapt to a warming planet. This includes direct technical work, such as energy modelling, conservation biology, and sustainable agriculture, as well as the functions every organisation needs: fundraising, communications, software development, and programme management.

    The range of roles is wide. Climate teams include UX designers building carbon dashboards, lobbyists drafting clean-energy legislation, and data engineers working on satellite deforestation alerts. Remote work means people can contribute from anywhere.

    Key skills employers look for

    • GHG accounting and LCA — Measuring carbon footprints across supply chains (GHG Protocol, ISO 14064).
    • Geospatial and remote sensing — GIS, Google Earth Engine, satellite imagery for land-use monitoring.
    • Policy and regulation — Paris Agreement, EU Taxonomy, climate disclosure rules.
    • Data science and modelling — Python, R, climate models (CMIP6), scenario analysis.
    • Stakeholder engagement — Grant writing, coalition building, community outreach, especially for NGOs.
    • Project management — Coordinating distributed teams across time zones is central to remote climate work.

    Where to start

    If you're moving into climate work, start by finding the overlap between your current expertise and what the sector needs.

    • Software engineers: Target climate-tech startups building monitoring platforms.
    • Marketing professionals: Help NGOs run and grow their campaigns.
    • Finance background: Look at climate investment and green bonds.
    • Policy specialists: International climate frameworks need analysts.

    Next steps: Volunteer for a climate organisation, complete a short course (Terra.do and Climate Draft are popular), and contribute to open-source climate projects on GitHub. These show hiring managers that your interest is real.

    Frequently asked questions

    What qualifications do I need for a remote climate job?

    Requirements depend on the occupation and employer. Compare the required skills, experience, education and work authorization in the specific vacancy. A scientific role may need specialist training, while communications, operations and software roles have different requirements. A climate mission alone does not establish a degree or certification requirement.

    Can I work in climate without a science degree?

    Some climate-related roles focus on communications, fundraising, operations, policy or software rather than scientific research. Check the specific vacancy: a role title or mission alone does not prove that you meet its education, experience or location requirements.

    What is the typical salary range for remote climate jobs?

    Climate work spans scientific, engineering, policy and programme roles with different pay structures. Compare the specific employer’s advertised currency, pay period, seniority, work location and benefits. A national occupational wage statistic is not a remote-climate or entry-level salary benchmark.

    For occupation-specific context, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics environmental-scientist profile describes one U.S. occupation, not worldwide remote climate work.
