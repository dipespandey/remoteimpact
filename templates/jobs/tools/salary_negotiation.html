7 Salary Negotiation Tips for Remote Workers

  1. Research market rates: Use sites like Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, and Payscale to know your worth in your specific role, industry, and location.
  2. Quantify your impact: Frame achievements with numbers — revenue generated, costs saved, users served, or projects delivered.
  3. Negotiate the full package: Remote roles often include stipends for home office, internet, coworking, wellness, and professional development. Don't leave these on the table.
  4. Practice out loud: Rehearse your script with a friend or record yourself. Hearing your own words builds confidence for the real conversation.
  5. Anchor high (but realistic): Your first number sets the range. Start at the top of your research-backed range so there's room to meet in the middle.
  6. Don't reveal your current salary first: In many jurisdictions it's illegal for employers to ask. Redirect to your target range based on market data.
  7. Get it in writing: Once you reach agreement verbally, ask for a revised offer letter before accepting. Remote work terms (location flexibility, timezone expectations) should be explicit.
