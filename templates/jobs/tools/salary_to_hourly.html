How to Convert Salary to Hourly Rate

The formula is straightforward:

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (Hours per Week × Weeks per Year)

For a standard full-time schedule of 40 hours/week and 52 weeks/year, you work 2,080 hours annually. So a $50,000 salary equals roughly $24.04/hour.

Adjusting for Time Off

If you get 2 weeks of unpaid vacation, change "Weeks per Year" to 50. This gives a more accurate picture of your effective hourly rate, since you're earning the same salary over fewer working weeks.

Why This Matters for Remote Workers

When comparing a salaried position to freelance or contract work, understanding your hourly equivalent helps you make fair comparisons. Remember that freelancers typically need to charge 25-50% more per hour to cover self-employment taxes, benefits, and unpaid time off.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert my annual salary to an hourly rate?

Divide your annual salary by the total number of hours you work in a year. For a standard 40-hour week with 52 weeks, that's 2,080 hours. For example, a $60,000 salary ÷ 2,080 = $28.85/hour.

How do I convert an hourly rate to annual salary?

Multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work per week, then multiply by 52 weeks. For example, $30/hour × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $62,400/year.

What is a good hourly rate for remote work?

It varies widely by field and experience. In the US, remote work hourly rates typically range from $20-$75/hour for most roles, with specialized tech and consulting roles going higher. Use this calculator to compare offers effectively.

Should I include overtime in the calculation?

If you regularly work overtime, increase the "Hours per Week" field to reflect your actual hours. This gives you a more realistic effective hourly rate—which may be lower than you expect for salaried roles.

How does the tax rate field work?

The optional tax rate provides an estimate of your after-tax earnings. Enter your approximate total tax rate (federal + state + local) to see net pay figures. This is an estimate—consult a tax professional for exact numbers.
