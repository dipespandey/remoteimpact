What makes a good impact cover letter

  • Open with a reason, not a formula. Skip "I'm writing to express my interest." Start with a piece of their work you know, or a result of yours that matches the role.
  • Use numbers you can defend. "Cut donor churn by 23%" or "managed a $1.2M grant portfolio serving 4,000 people" says more than "improved outcomes".
  • Use the posting's own words. If they say "program design", don't write "curriculum development". It shows you read the post and helps with screening software.
  • Show mission fit with something you did. A project, a volunteer role or a career decision says more than "I'm passionate about climate".
  • Keep it under 250 words. Two or three proof points are enough. For remote roles, add your time zone and the hours you can overlap with the team.

Drafts from this tool follow the same rules: a short opening, three proof points from your highlights, and under 220 words. It can get details wrong, so read every line before you send it.

Notes by type of role

  • Nonprofit and NGO jobs

    Name the grants, programs or community outcomes you worked on.

  • Climate jobs

    Pair your technical skills with an emissions, energy or land result you can measure.

  • Social enterprise jobs

    Show you can grow revenue and the social result at the same time.

  • Humanitarian jobs

    Mention field context, languages and how you worked with partners on the ground.

  • Fundraising jobs

    Give amounts raised, donor types and your renewal rate.

  • Changing careers into impact work

    Translate past results into the role's terms and say plainly why you are switching.
