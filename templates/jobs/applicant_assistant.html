Tips for Writing Great Cover Letters

Whether you use our AI generator or write from scratch, these tips will help your application stand out.

1

Open with a specific hook, not a generic intro

Skip "I'm writing to express my interest." Instead, lead with a metric, a shared value, or a specific reason you're drawn to this organization's mission.

2

Quantify your impact with real numbers

Replace vague claims like "improved outcomes" with concrete metrics: "Reduced donor churn by 23%" or "Managed a $1.2M grant portfolio serving 4,000 beneficiaries."

3

Mirror the job description's language

Use keywords from the JD naturally. If they say "program design," don't say "curriculum development." This helps with ATS systems and shows you've read the posting carefully.

4

Show mission alignment, don't just state it

Don't just say "I'm passionate about climate." Describe a specific project, volunteer experience, or decision that demonstrates your commitment to the cause.

5

Keep it under 250 words

Hiring managers skim. A concise, high-signal cover letter with 2-3 proof points beats a full-page essay. Respect their time and they'll respect your application.

6

For remote roles, address logistics proactively

Mention your timezone, overlap hours with the team, and experience with remote collaboration tools. This removes friction and shows you understand distributed work.
